# FAIL

The Free AI License (FAIL) was created specifically for Artificial Intelligence projects.

Its ecosystem includes documentation, specifications, compliance guidance, optional project registration and machine-readable metadata to simplify adoption across AI applications, models, datasets and agents.

---

# MIT License

The MIT License is one of the simplest and most widely adopted open-source licenses.

It grants broad permissions to use, modify and distribute software with very few obligations beyond preserving the copyright notice and license text.

---

# Apache License 2.0

Apache 2.0 is a permissive license that includes an explicit patent grant and additional protections related to patents and contributions.

It is commonly used for large collaborative software projects.

---

# GNU General Public License v3 (GPLv3)

GPLv3 is a strong copyleft license.

Projects distributed under GPLv3 generally require derivative works that are distributed to remain under the GPL, helping ensure that source code remains available under the same license terms.

---

# Mozilla Public License 2.0 (MPL 2.0)

MPL 2.0 is a file-level copyleft license.

It allows proprietary and open-source code to coexist in many cases while requiring modifications to MPL-covered files to remain under the MPL.

# When FAIL May Be a Good Fit

FAIL may be appropriate when a project wants:

- A license intended for AI-related projects.
- Optional ecosystem documentation and metadata.
- Guidance tailored to AI models, datasets, agents and services.
- An open and collaborative community around AI licensing.

---

# Choosing a License

Different licenses serve different goals.

Before selecting a license, consider:

- Whether commercial use should be allowed.
- Whether derivative works should have additional obligations.
- Whether patent provisions are important.
- Whether the project would benefit from AI-focused documentation and tooling.

There is no single "best" license for every project.
